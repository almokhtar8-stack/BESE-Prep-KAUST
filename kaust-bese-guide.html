Phase 1 — Foundations

The 7-Step Application Roadmap

01
Meet the Hard Requirements Required
Before anything else, confirm you qualify. KAUST screens hard on these.
  • BSc in Biology, Microbiology, Biochemistry, Bioengineering, or related field
  • GPA: aim for 3.5+/4.0 — below this needs strong research to offset
  • English proficiency: IELTS 6.5+ or TOEFL 79+ (waiver if degree was English-medium)
  • GRE not required — but a strong score is a plus if you have it
02
Build a Research Profile High Impact
KAUST selects researchers, not just students. Any research experience elevates your profile dramatically.
  • Published paper, preprint (Research Square, bioRxiv), or thesis chapter
  • Lab work, capstone projects, final year thesis — document and quantify everything
  • GitHub with bioinformatics analysis or code (R, Python, bash pipelines)
  • Wet lab skills: PCR, cell culture, NGS library prep, CRISPR — list them explicitly
03
Contact a PI Before Applying Most Critical
This is the single highest-leverage action in the entire process. See the PI Outreach section below for full guidance and a template.
  • Browse KAUST BESE faculty: bese.kaust.edu.sa
  • Identify 2–3 PIs with active projects matching your exact interests
  • Read their most recent paper before emailing
  • A PI who invites you to apply dramatically increases your odds of admission
04
Enter the KAUST Circle High Impact
KAUST rewards familiarity. Being known before you apply makes a real difference.
  • KAUST Academy — specialization programs (Bioinformatics, AI, Cybersecurity, Energy, Water) for university students; Women in STEM track also available
  • KAUST Elevate — on-campus training for recent Saudi graduates; Science & Engineering track puts you on campus before you're a student
  • Winter Enrichment Program (WEP) — annual two-week event open to outside guests; attending puts you in the room with faculty and current students
  • Follow and engage with KAUST researchers on LinkedIn and ResearchGate; attend KAUST-affiliated events at KAIMRC, KFSHRC, or SDAIA
05
Write a Targeted SOP + Strong CV Required
Your SOP is your research pitch, not your autobiography. Make it specific and scientific.
  • Name the PI you want to work with and explain why their work aligns with yours
  • State your research question or interest in one precise sentence
  • CV: wet lab skills, tools, any bioinformatics coursework, certifications, GitHub
  • Certifications that help: Coursera Bioinformatics, EMBL courses, NGS sequencing workshops
06
Secure Strong Reference Letters High Impact
3 letters required. Quality matters far more than seniority of the referee.
  • At least 2 from research supervisors who can speak to your lab competence — not just GPA
  • A letter from someone who collaborates with KAUST or has KAUST ties is a significant edge
  • Give referees your SOP and CV so their letters directly reinforce your narrative
07
Prepare Hard for the Interview Most Critical
KAUST MS interviews are research-focused. They want to know if you can think like a scientist.
  • Be ready to discuss your thesis or capstone project in depth — methods, results, limitations
  • Prepare 2–3 research questions you want to explore at KAUST
  • Know the PI's recent papers well enough to ask intelligent questions
  • Use the preparation resources linked below
Phase 2 — PI Strategy

The PI Outreach Playbook

Why This Changes Everything

Most applicants skip this step. The ones who get in often don't. A PI who knows your name before your application lands is an internal advocate. KAUST admission is heavily lab-driven — the PI has real influence over who joins their group, and by extension, who gets accepted.

Subject: Prospective MS Student — Interest in [Lab Focus] Research Dear Professor [Last Name], I am writing to express my interest in pursuing an MS in Bioscience / Bioengineering at KAUST, with the hope of joining your lab. I recently read your paper "[Paper Title, Year]" and found your approach to [specific method or finding] directly relevant to a question I have been developing during my undergraduate research in [your field]. During my BSc at [University], I worked on [brief project description], where I gained experience in [relevant techniques]. I am particularly interested in exploring [specific research angle] — a direction I believe aligns with your current work on [lab focus]. I am planning to apply for the Fall 2026 intake and would greatly appreciate the opportunity to briefly discuss whether there may be a fit between my background and your lab's needs. Thank you for your time. Sincerely, [Your Full Name] [BSc Institution · GPA · LinkedIn/GitHub]
